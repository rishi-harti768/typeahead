Trending & Recency-Aware Ranking

Targeting: System Design Interview Success • Duration: 15 mins

In a basic typeahead system, suggestions are sorted strictly by their overall historical search count. While this works well for evergreen queries (e.g., "java tutorial" or "how to tie a tie"), it fails catastrophically when real-world events trigger sudden viral surges of interest.

If the Super Bowl is happening *right now*, users typing su expect to see "super bowl live". But if "super bowl live" only has 5,000 historical searches compared to "sushi recipe" which has 500,000 historical searches over five years, the viral trend is completely buried.

How do we elevate active trends without permanently over-ranking queries that are only popular for a brief period?

The Recency Challenge: If we simply boost anything searched recently, typo-queries or short-term spam will dominate your suggestions. We need a mathematical model to decay trends gracefully.

The Solution: Exponential Time Decay

To balance historical stability with instant viral freshness, we compute a recency-aware score using **Exponential Time Decay**.

We separate each query's popularity score into two components: 

Score = HistoricalBaseline + (SpikeCount * 0.5 ^ (TimePassed / HalfLife))

Where:

  • HistoricalBaseline: The long-term, stable popularity count of the search query.
  • SpikeCount: The volume of fresh searches recorded during the current temporal event.
  • TimePassed: The hours or days that have elapsed since the peak of the search event.
  • HalfLife: The time window in which a trend's weight halves (e.g., 12 hours).

Why this is elegant: During a viral spike, SpikeCount dominates, vaulting the query to the absolute top of suggestions. As the event passes and TimePassed increases, the spike decays exponentially. Within a few days, the score naturally settles back to its HistoricalBaseline without manual database intervention!

Cache Invalidation: Because scores decay continuously, how do we prevent stale cache hits? We implement **passive expiration** using short TTLs on cache entries (e.g., 5 mins) or **active updates** via streaming cron-jobs (e.g., Flink) that refresh top Tries periodically.
